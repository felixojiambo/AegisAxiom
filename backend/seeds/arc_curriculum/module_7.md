This module's reading is short. The quiz and the final test are where you'll prove you can synthesize. But there are a few principles worth holding in your head before you take the capstone.

## The integration mindset

The first principle is that the frameworks don't fire one at a time — they fire together, and they reinforce each other.

Imagine you're 15 minutes into a Call 1 and the prospect says: *"We use SAP for ERP and Excel for everything else. Our QC team spends 3 hours a day cross-referencing inspection reports against shipment records. There's no integration between SAP and our QC system."*

A new rep might pick one framework and apply it. *"That sounds like a Data-Heavy Workflow."* True, but incomplete.

A trained AI Recruiter hears the same sentence and runs all the frameworks at once: *Data state is Siloed (SAP plus Excel as glue is the smoking gun). The QC workflow is Data-Heavy (cross-referencing across multiple sources). The AEGISAXIOM approach is an Automated Workflow archetype, probably with Pulsar handling the rules-based matching. Rough MMI hypothesis: TSS around 3-4 because it's back-office, IE around 2 because it's tractable, Time 4-5 because 3 hours/day is high, Cost 3-4 because it's burning real labor. Score probably lands in the 25-35 range. My next Power Question should probe volume and integration depth.*

That's the mindset. Every sentence the client speaks is information for multiple frameworks at once, and your job is to process it all in parallel. With practice this becomes automatic. You stop thinking about which framework to apply — you just listen, and the classifications happen on their own.

## When frameworks conflict

Sometimes the frameworks point in different directions, and you have to make a judgment call. A workflow might be Data-Heavy AND Repeated. A prospect might be mostly Visible but with one Blind pocket. A workflow might score high on Quality but low on Time. When this happens, don't panic and don't try to force a single answer. Surface the complexity to the client.

The right move when frameworks conflict is to name the tension out loud: *"This workflow has the volume signature of a Repeated Workflow, but the cross-referencing requirement makes it Data-Heavy. We can solve both with the same agent, but it changes the implementation effort. Let me ask a couple more questions to see which is dominant."* Clients respect this kind of transparency. It signals that you understand their work in detail rather than fitting it into a slogan.

## The most common failure modes

There are three patterns that trip up new AI Recruiters in their first real engagements. Knowing them in advance is half the battle.

**The first failure is over-promising in Call 1.** The client gets excited, you get excited, and you find yourself committing to scope before you understand the data state. By Call 3 you're trying to deliver workflows the underlying data can't support. The fix: discipline. Call 1 is for listening, not selling. Stay in diagnosis mode no matter how tempting it is to jump ahead.

**The second failure is treating Vega as optional.** New reps sometimes try to do all the post-call analysis themselves, thinking they'll save time or impress the team. They miss things. They produce shallower analyses than Vega. They lose the consistency that the methodology depends on. Use Vega every time. That's the whole point of the partnership.

**The third failure is letting the client define the scope.** Clients describe their workflows the way they currently run them, with all the human handoffs and irrational legacy decisions. If you scope what the client describes, you're scoping the current state — which is exactly what they're paying you to change. Use first-principles thinking. Strip the human handoffs. Show them what the workflow could look like if you started from scratch. That's where the real value lives.

## What a Gold Standard report looks like

When all three calls are done and Vega has finalized the AI Employee Matrix, the deliverable that goes to Orion is what we call the Gold Standard report. It contains:

- The list of pain points from Call 1, in the client's own words
- The data state diagnosis with justification
- The full AI Employee Matrix: every workflow, with its lens classification, source applications, manual hours, stakeholders, and MMI score
- The AEGISAXIOM agents recommended for each workflow
- Resolved gaps from Call 2 (with notes on how they were resolved)
- The MMI rankings, sorted descending
- Stretch opportunities the client mentioned but that weren't in scope for the immediate engagement

This is what Orion consumes to draft the SOW. The quality of the SOW is directly proportional to the quality of the Gold Standard report. Garbage in, garbage out. Excellence in, polished SOW out.
